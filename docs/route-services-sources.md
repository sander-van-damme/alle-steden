# Voorzieningen langs een fietsroute

Source: [Pin je punt (output and reuse conditions), Toerisme Vlaanderen](https://toerismevlaanderen.be/nl/thema/fietsen/pinjepunt-output). The official publisher invites reuse of its public WFS exports and requires visible, linked [© OpenStreetMap-bijdragers](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). The publisher describes a nightly export from OpenStreetMap; this is not a guarantee that individual features were recently surveyed.

The router and the service search are independent. Only for a chosen qualifying route intersecting Flanders, the service adapter sends the padded route bounding box (not the waypoints or the actual route line) to the two fixed WFS 2.0 layers at `https://geodata.toerismevlaanderen.be/geoserver/poi/wfs`. Both were verified to accept `CRS:84` longitude/latitude BBOX and return GeoJSON, HTTP 200 and `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` with the production Origin. Two calls total, maximum 140 raw features per layer, no automatic pagination or route changes. The client discards points beyond 650 m as measured from the actual routed geometry and selects up to six nearby results per category. For separately routed legs it does not join any gap with a synthetic road. Limits: maximum six legs, 100 route km, 25,000 points, or a 650 km² bounding box. A partial or empty result does not prove the area has no facilities, especially outside the largely Flemish dataset.

Only the bicycle pump/repair and public toilet layers are in scope. The adapter requires explicit public access and consistent point coordinates; skips a known non-ground level, outright closures, reported broken stations or repair stations without published tools or pump, and distinguishes urinals. E-bike chargers, rentals, seating and picnic tables are not silently included: the tested layers did not expose dependable public-access restrictions for those features, and charging compatibility and availability are separate concerns. No hours, fees, current access/operation or accessible access are asserted by the adapter. The provider's link leads to the official source, while a per-result OpenStreetMap link locates the point without guessing the OSM object type from the WFS ID.

A separate detour adapter (not activated by the current planner) can recalculate the actual containing leg through an allowed nearby point, accepts a maximum 75 m pin snap, returns the signed difference in BRouter distance/time, and does not change the itinerary. A pin within that tolerance is not evidence of an accessible final entrance.

Live audit, 2026-11-24: the BRouter Gent → Eeklo route measured 21.307 km. The actual Pin je punt queries with production Origin produced six public-labelled toilet results (selection capped) and two public-labelled bicycle service points. One pump and repair point about 436 m away from the route as the crow flies was separately routed at +895 m / +2 min 29 s with a 1 m pin snap. Provider response times measured through the test network were approximately 7.3–7.5 s for the two WFS layers and 5.8 s for that one optional reroute.

Aat adds two local fallback points: Square Saint-Julien and Esplanade are identified by the [Visit Ath city map](https://visitath.be/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/OTATH_plan-Ath_570x320mm_2025_WEB.pdf) and matched to OpenStreetMap nodes [13200724524](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/13200724524) and [12060857285](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/12060857285), verified through the OSM read API on 26 November 2026. Coordinates © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. These are two points, not a full Walloon search; the proximity test is local, uses the actual route, and never asserts opening, price or continued access. The second Esplanade node identified as a urinal is not added.
